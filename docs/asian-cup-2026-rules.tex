\section{Tournament Overview}

\subsection{Dates and participants}

The draw will take place on \term{August 1, 2026}. The tournament will run from
\term{August 3 through September 13, 2026}.

The seven participating teams, listed in the order of the National Team Ranking
used for the draw, are:

  4 & Thailand & 1598 & & &\\

\subsection{Competition structure}

\begin{itemize}
  \item All seven teams compete in a \term{single group}.
  \item Each team plays \term{four matches against four different opponents}.
  \item The Group Stage schedule is created through the balanced draw described
        in Section~\ref{sec:draw}.
  \item After the Group Stage:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item \term{1st vs 2nd} --- Championship Match;
      \item \term{3rd vs 4th} --- Bronze Medal Match.
    \end{itemize}
  \item Teams finishing fifth through seventh retain their relative Group Stage
        positions in the final ranking.
\end{itemize}

\section{Eligibility and Team Requirements}

\subsection{Eligibility}

Each team must consist of players from the same country, or from a single region
where Carcassonne is published in its own language. Only one team per
country or region is allowed. Team members must have been born in, or currently
live in, the country or region they represent.

\subsection{Registration}

The captain registers the team through the \PlayerHub. When building the team
roster, the captain selects players who have already been added to their
association or creates records for players who have not yet been added.
Captains are responsible for keeping this information accurate and up to date
throughout the tournament.

\subsection{Team composition}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A team must have at least \term{8 players} and no more than
        \term{10 players}.
  \item At least eight team members must play at least one duel during the Group
        Stage. A team that fails to meet this requirement loses one match win
        (\term{MW}) in the final Group Stage standings.
  \item Each team must appoint one captain.
  \item During the Group Stage only, a team may add players up to the ten-player
        limit or replace players. A player who has already played in at least
        one match may not be replaced. Every change requires prior notice to
        the organizers.
  \item Each team must have access to a Premium BGA account capable of creating
        the tournament match. The account holder does not have to be in that
        match's lineup.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Captain's responsibility}

The captain must ensure that every team member understands and accepts these
rules and later organizer updates. The captain handles team communication,
scheduling, lineups, and results.

\section{Scheduling and Lineups}

\subsection{Official schedule}

The organizer-published schedule determines the Group Stage rounds and the
dates of the Championship and Bronze Medal Matches. Captains arrange the exact
date and time of each assigned match within the permitted round window.

All submitted dates and times must use \term{UTC+8}. Captains may use
\href{https://savvytime.com/converter}{Savvy Time} to avoid conversion errors.

\subsection{Scheduling a match}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Captains must agree on and submit their match date and time by
        \term{23:59 UTC+8 on Wednesday} of the applicable match week through
        the \PlayerHub.
  \item A match may be played earlier, provided that the date, time, and both
        lineups are submitted at least 24 hours before it begins.
  \item If no date and time are submitted by the deadline, the match is
        automatically scheduled for \term{Sunday at 19:00 UTC+8}.
  \item Captains should reply promptly to scheduling messages, even when a final
        time has not yet been agreed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Submitting lineups}

Each captain submits a lineup of five players, assigned to positions 1--5,
through the \PlayerHub\ at least \term{24 hours before the match}. The
submitted lineups are then published on \TournamentPage.

Position 1 on one team plays Position 1 on the other team, and so on through
Position 5. After submission, no more than one player may be changed, and only
because of a major unforeseen event with organizer approval.

Failure to submit a lineup on time may be penalized as a match loss:
\term{0--5 duels and 0--10 games}.

\section{Balanced Group Stage Draw}
\label{sec:draw}

\subsection{Ranking basis}

The draw uses the current \RankingPage. Instead of producing a completely
random schedule, it gives every team a balanced set of opponents while
preserving randomness.

For each team, the other six teams are divided into:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the \term{Top 3} --- the three highest-rated available opponents; and
  \item the \term{Lower 3} --- the three lowest-rated available opponents.
\end{itemize}

Each team is drawn to play:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \term{two opponents from its Top 3}; and
  \item \term{two opponents from its Lower 3}.
\end{itemize}

Because the opponent sets are calculated separately for every team, a pairing
may belong to different categories from the two teams' perspectives.

\begin{notebox}
\textbf{Examples.}
For Japan, the Top 3 are China, Hong Kong, and Thailand; the Lower 3 are
Taiwan, Australia, and Vietnam. For Taiwan, the Top 3 are Japan, China, and
Hong Kong; the Lower 3 are Thailand, Australia, and Vietnam.
\end{notebox}

\subsection{Draw validity}

A schedule is valid only if all of the following are true:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item every team has exactly four opponents;
  \item no two teams meet more than once in the Group Stage;
  \item every scheduled pairing is reciprocal;
  \item from each team's own perspective, exactly two opponents are from its
        Top 3 and exactly two are from its Lower 3.
\end{enumerate}

This prevents any team from receiving an exceptionally difficult or
exceptionally easy schedule while still leaving room for randomness in the
draw.

\section{Match Format}

\subsection{Five simultaneous duels}

A team match consists of \term{five duels}, normally played simultaneously.
Each duel is a best-of-three encounter between one player from each team.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The first player to win two games wins the duel.
  \item If a player wins the first two games, the duel ends 2--0 and no third
        result is recorded.
  \item The team that wins at least three of the five duels wins the match.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{BGA tournament settings}

Matches are played in BGA tournament mode with \term{15 minutes per player}.
The starting player does not necessarily alternate between games, so the same
player may start two or all three games of a duel.

If a duel has already been decided after two games but BGA launches a third
game, both players must enter it and jointly abandon it.

\subsection{Tied game score}

Every game must produce a winner for tournament purposes. If the final board
score is tied, the player who \term{did not start the game} is the winner.

\section{Group Stage Standings}
\label{sec:standings}

\subsection{Recorded statistics}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\bfseries}p{16mm}X >{\bfseries}p{16mm}X}
  \toprule
  MP & Matches Played & MW & Matches Won\\
  DW & Duels Won & DL & Duels Lost\\
  GW & Games Won & GL & Games Lost\\
  DD & Duels Difference ($DW-DL$) & GD & Games Difference ($GW-GL$)\\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

One match win is recorded for each team match won, one duel win for each duel
won, and one game win for each game won.

\subsection{Tie-break order}

Final Group Stage positions are determined in the following order:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \term{MW (Matches Won)} --- number of team matches won.
  \item \term{DD (Duels Difference)} --- difference between Duels Won and
        Duels Lost.
  \item \term{DW (Duels Won)} --- total number of duels won.
  \item \term{GD (Games Difference)} --- difference between Games Won and
        Games Lost.
  \item \term{GW (Games Won)} --- total number of games won.
  \item \term{OMW (Opponents' Matches Won)} --- total number of team matches
        won by all opponents faced during the Group Stage.
  \item \term{ODD (Opponents' Duels Difference)} --- combined difference
        between DW and DL of all opponents faced during the Group Stage.
  \item \term{ODW (Opponents' Duels Won)} --- total number of duels won by all
        opponents faced during the Group Stage.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{notebox}
\textbf{Buchholz principle.}
OMW, ODD, and ODW measure the results achieved by the opponents a team actually
faced. They reward strength of schedule and are applied only after the team's
own match, duel, and game results.
\end{notebox}

\subsection{Medal matches and final positions}

The first- and second-placed teams contest the Championship Match. Its winner
finishes first and its loser finishes second. The third- and fourth-placed teams
contest the Bronze Medal Match. Its winner finishes third and its loser finishes
fourth.

\section{Clock Rules}

If a player runs out of time during a game, that player loses the game. This is
a loss for that game only, not automatically for the entire duel.

A player has run out of time when the time shown after completing a turn and
synchronizing with the server is zero or negative. If possible, the player
should concede so that BGA awards the win correctly. If there is doubt, the red
``time penalty'' clock shown in the game results is decisive. If both players
run out of time, the player who ran out first loses.

Players should reduce connection risks by arranging a reliable internet
connection and, where possible, keeping a backup connection such as a mobile
hotspot available. Any duel affected by a serious technical issue must be
completed as soon as possible and reported to the organizers.

\section{Courtesy and Fair Play}

\subsection{Player conduct}

Players and spectators must remain courteous and respectful. Captains should
encourage prompt communication and avoid waiting until the final moment to
resolve scheduling issues.

\subsection{Discarded tiles}

When BGA automatically discards a tile that cannot be placed legally, the
opponent may not notice it. The player should inform the opponent in the game
chat whenever this happens.

\subsection{Unauthorized assistance}

Players must play their own games without advice, coaching, or outside
assistance. Tile-counting and memory aids---including apps, spreadsheets, notes,
or pen and paper---are strictly forbidden. Players are also responsible for
avoiding unsolicited help.

An individual or team using inappropriate assistance may be removed from the
tournament and may also be excluded from future editions.

\subsection{Conceding}

Except when a player has run out of time or when these rules explicitly require
abandoning an unnecessary third game, games must be played to completion.

\section{Results, Absences, and Incidents}

\subsection{Reporting results}

Results are recorded automatically from BGA data. Within \term{24 hours after
the match ends}, both captains must review them in the \PlayerHub. Either
captain may submit a required correction there for approval by the opposing
captain; unresolved discrepancies must be referred to the organizers.

\subsection{How incidents are recorded}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \term{Normal game:} the final score and winner are imported
        automatically from BGA. No action is required unless the imported data
        are incorrect.
  \item \term{Tied board score:} the non-starting player is the game winner.
        Submit a correction if the automatic result does not reflect this rule.
  \item \term{Time loss:} the game is a win for the opponent. Submit a
        correction if necessary.
  \item \term{No-show:} if a player has not appeared within 15 minutes of the
        scheduled time, the opponent wins the duel 2--0; the captain must submit
        the required correction through the Player Hub.
  \item \term{Duel decided in two games:} no third-game result is recorded.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Before play begins}

Players must join promptly and verify the tournament settings before play. BGA
may close a tournament place when a player does not enter within the platform's
joining window. Once play has begun, claims based on settings that could have
been checked beforehand may be rejected.

\section{Duties, Disputes, and Prizes}

\subsection{Duties of team captains}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Ensure that all players understand and follow the rules and organizer
        updates.
  \item Remind team members to behave respectfully as players and spectators.
  \item Communicate with the opposing captain and tournament organizers.
  \item Submit match dates, times, and lineups, and review automatically
        recorded results, by their deadlines.
  \item Notify the organizers about approved roster changes and significant
        match incidents.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dispute Arbitration Zone}

The Dispute Arbitration Zone (DAZ) considers disputes and determines the
appropriate resolution. The organizers may also act against players or teams
engaging in unfair play or conduct contrary to these rules.

When submitting a dispute, provide the match, duel, game link, relevant times,
screenshots, and a concise description of the issue. Organizer contact details
and the official communication channel will be published with the tournament
materials.

\subsection{Prizes}

The winning team will be crowned the \term{Carcassonne Asian Cup 2026 Champion}
and recognized as the leading online national team in Asia and Oceania for this
edition.

\subsection{Questions}

Questions about these rules should be sent through the official organizer
contact channel. Tournament announcements and updates will be published on
\TournamentPage.
